Chapter 3

Version 1 scores 6.4, and revenue questions score lowest. Priya opens one of them: “What was the total order amount per customer region?” The checks say the joins differ from the expected SQL. Sidekick wrote a query, but not the right one. The query is the symptom. To find the cause, Priya needs to see what happened inside.

What a step gives you

A step is a named stage of your pipeline: picking the tables, writing the query, checking it, calling the model. Mark the stages and, for every question, the dashboard shows:

  • How long each step took: the “where the time goes” bar on the version page.
  • What went in and came out of a step, when you use the decorator: the tables chosen, the prompt, the draft query.
  • Notes you attach: intent, tool called, tables considered… anything you want next to the question.

For Sidekick that means one look at the step that picks the tables shows the cause: it picked orders but not customers, so the query could not join them. The model did its best with what it was given.

Nothing happens in production

Outside an evaluation, step() and note() record nothing and add almost no overhead, so the same code can ship. Arguments named like secrets are never stored.

Steps are optional: without them you still get every check and score. There are two ways to write a step. They look similar and differ in what they record: that is the next page. After that, what the checks and scores mean.
